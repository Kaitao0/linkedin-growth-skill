# Bilingual Strategy (PT / EN)

How to handle Portuguese and English content for users targeting both local (BR) and international audiences.

---

## Core principle

**One LinkedIn profile, two languages — never two profiles.**

LinkedIn natively supports multiple language versions of the same profile. Creating a second account is a violation of LinkedIn's User Agreement and risks both accounts being restricted. Always use the built-in multilingual feature.

How the user enables it (walk them through if needed):

1. Open profile → click the pencil to edit the intro section.
2. Scroll to "Profile in another language" / "Perfil em outro idioma".
3. Pick English.
4. Fill in: First name, Last name, Headline, About, and per-role descriptions.

The user's connections see the profile in their LinkedIn UI language. International recruiters searching in English will find the EN version surface in search.

---

## When to recommend EN setup

Recommend the user set up EN if any of the following is true:

- They want to be considered for international roles (remote or relocation).
- Their company has international operations and they want internal visibility.
- They publish technical content that has a global audience (engineering, AI, design, product).
- They sell services or products to international clients.

Do not recommend EN if:

- The user's niche is purely local (local government, regional retail with no international supplier exposure, regulated professions tied to BR jurisdiction like notary).
- The user explicitly says they have zero interest in international audience.

## Translating profile sections

The EN version is **not** a literal translation. It is a culturally adapted version that keeps the same brand essence.

### Headline
- Convert role titles to international standards: "Coordenador" → "Manager" or "Lead" depending on actual scope (not "Coordinator", which reads as junior in EN).
- Keywords must match what international recruiters search for (use LinkedIn's job search to validate terms).
- Keep the same value proposition, but rewrite — not translate.

Example:
- PT: "Gestor e Liderança em Tecnologia | Coordenador | Estratégia Digital"
- EN: "Tech Lead at Brazil's largest wholesale retailer | ERP Integration & Automation | TOTVS, n8n, Oracle"

### About
- Keep the same hook strategy, but rewrite the hook in idiomatic English.
- Adjust company references: include a one-line gloss of who the company is ("Fort Atacadista, a wholesale retail chain operating across central Brazil").
- Adjust units, currencies, and date formats.
- Keep the same numbers and results.

### Experience
- Mirror the bullets but in active English voice.
- Localize tool names if they have international equivalents (TOTVS RM has no equivalent — keep as is and add a parenthetical).
- Use US date format unless targeting UK/EU specifically (Mon YYYY).

### Skills
- The skill names are the same database underneath (LinkedIn matches them across languages), but the user sees the names in their UI language. No translation needed in most cases — just ensure the skills are added.

## Posting strategy across languages

Three modes. Decide per post.

### Mode A — PT only
Use when:
- Topic is BR-specific (local market, regulation, language-bound nuance).
- Audience explicitly is BR peers and clients.
- Content references BR cultural moments (Carnaval, eleições, novela).

### Mode B — EN only
Use when:
- Topic is for international recruiters specifically.
- Content is technical and the audience is global (open-source, AI research, international tooling).
- The user is in active international job search.

### Mode C — Bilingual in same post
Use when:
- Technical content with global appeal AND BR audience interest.
- Major personal milestone the user wants both audiences to see.
- Industry insight that translates cleanly.

Format for bilingual posts:

```
🇧🇷 [PT version, full post following content_strategy.md]

—

🇺🇸 [EN version, full post]

#hashtags (mix PT/EN)
```

Caveat: bilingual posts get ~30% less engagement than single-language posts because the algorithm has to choose which audience to serve and the read time is doubled. Use sparingly — once or twice a month.

### Mode D — Same post, alternate days (recommended default)
The cleanest approach for users with both audiences:

- Tuesday morning: PT version of the post.
- Thursday morning: EN version of the post (rewritten, not translated).

This way each audience gets a native-feeling post in their language, and the algorithm serves each one to the right people.

---

## Calendar split

For a 12-posts/month user with bilingual setup:

- 7 PT-only posts.
- 4 EN-only posts.
- 1 bilingual post (for major topics).

For a user actively job-seeking internationally:

- 4 PT-only.
- 7 EN-only.
- 1 bilingual.

Adjust based on which audience is responding more.

## Hashtag handling

Hashtags are language-specific. Don't mix in single-language posts.

- PT post: #IntegraçãoDeSistemas, #VarejoTech, #ERP
- EN post: #SystemsIntegration, #RetailTech, #ERP (some are universal)

Build two hashtag banks per user: one PT, one EN. Save in `nicho_e_publico.md`.

## Common mistakes to flag

- Literal translation that produces awkward English ("realize" used in the BR sense of "perform/do").
- Job titles inflated in EN ("Senior Director" when the actual role is mid-level).
- Mentioning Brazilian holidays in EN posts without context.
- Using BR idioms translated literally ("piece of cake" might land, "sopa no mel" definitely won't).
- Currency mismatches (R$ in EN posts confuses international readers — convert to USD in parentheses).
